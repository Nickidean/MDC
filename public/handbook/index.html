🔍 Child missing on site

  1. Shout "STOP" — everyone freezes. Immediate headcount of all children
  2. One adult straight to the water's edge — check the lakes first, always
  3. Remaining adults sweep the site — last seen point, dens, toilets, car park
  4. Not found within 5 minutes → ring 999. Don't wait, don't widen the search first
  5. Lead rings parents once police are called

Evacuation (fire or site emergency): calmly lead children to the 12 o'clock assembly point (car park, by the paddleboard store), take the register + contacts folder, count everyone against the register, nobody re-enters until it's safe.

💚Intro

I feel like children can really tell when adults put in the effort to look after them and really care. Some summer camps I've seen in the past have "big kids" looking after the children, which leads to them not being as attentive as they could be. I don't want that. We have three amazing camp leads who I feel the children will love, and we have enough help to really get to know them and make sure each of them has the best day.

The goal is simple: I want them to want to come back. This does not mean pandering to their every need and spoiling them, but it means giving them our attention, coaching them to try something new, talking to them, asking questions. Making them feel heard, pulling them up when needed and explaining why that was a bad choice, or likewise praising them when they really show something great. These could be little things that we notice.

If you have a group and they're loving an activity and want to continue, do that. Likewise, if they're not really enjoying something or want to try something else, do that too.

Any questions or feedback, please shout. It might be that something just doesn't work, and we need to adapt the plan.

🩹First aid
Every head bump = call home. No exceptions.

Every incident

Goes in the first aid book: who, what, when, treatment, who was told. Mention to parents at pick-up too.

Kit

  • First aid kit + ice packs in the tent, at the medical station table
  • Spare clothes for each child (parents send)
  • Trained first aiders on site: Nick & Annika (week 1), Nick (week 2)
  • Nick to check nearest defibrillator — location and confirm it's in working order

Epi-pens & meds

  • Check the allergy list — know who carries what
  • Epi-pens stay with the child's group, never locked away
  • All staff shown how to use one on day 1
  • Medication: labelled with name + dosage, given only with parental permission — confirmed on the register — except emergency action to protect life
  • Serious incidents → Nick or Annika immediately
Anaphylaxis: use the pen, then ring 999 — always, even if they seem to recover.

Sun & heat

  • Hats on, shade breaks, water pushed on hot days
  • Suncream: parents apply before drop-off + consent for staff to top up, confirmed on the register

Ticks

End-of-day reminder for children and parents to check for ticks — we're in the woods all day.

First-aid trained this week: Nick & Annika (week 1), Nick (week 2)

🔥Fire & evacuation
Assembly point: 12 o'clock point, car park, by the paddleboard store.
  • On the shout of "fire" or the whistle signal, everyone stops and heads straight to the assembly point, no detours, no collecting belongings
  • Camp leader takes the register and contacts folder on the way out
  • Headcount against the register at the assembly point, called out loud
  • Nobody re-enters the site until the lead says it's safe
  • Fire drill run on day 1 of each week, timed, so it's second nature if it's ever real
  • Fire extinguisher location: confirm and add
🤒Illness & infection control

A child feeling off can go from fine to not fine fast in the heat, so don't wait to act.

  • Unwell on arrival: if a child seems ill at drop-off, have a quiet word with the parent before they leave
  • Unwell during the day: bring them to the medical station, keep them comfortable, ring the parent
  • Sickness or diarrhoea: parent collects as soon as possible; child stays away from camp for 48 hours after symptoms stop
  • High temperature: parent collects; keep the child cool and hydrated while you wait
  • Any infectious condition (chickenpox, conjunctivitis, etc.): follow standard exclusion periods, ask the parent if unsure
  • Always write it up in the first aid book, same as any other incident
🛡️Safeguarding

Drop-off & pick-up

  1. Every child signed in (time + signature) and given a green wristband, then signed out — sheet records who is collecting today
  2. If someone we're not expecting from the register arrives to collect a child, ring the parent to check before releasing them. No confirmation, no release
  3. Waiting children: flagged waiting area, one named adult, one-to-one

Child not collected

  1. Two adults stay with the child — never one
  2. Work through every listed contact, log each attempt with times
  3. Nobody reached after 30–60 MIN — AGREE → ring children's social care duty line 01305 228558
  4. Never take the child home or leave them with a non-listed adult

Child doesn't arrive

  1. Check register at session start
  2. Ring parents straight away
  3. No answer? Try all contacts, log each attempt, escalate to lead

Child discloses something

  1. Listen — calm, no leading questions
  2. Reassure them they did the right thing by speaking up
  3. Never promise secrecy
  4. Write it down — their exact words, date, time
  5. Tell Nick (DSO) immediately (see printed staff sheet) — deputy if Nick unavailable: Annika
  6. Don't investigate or discuss with anyone else

Immediate danger? Call 999 first, Nick second. Written records go to Nick and are stored securely — DSO access only.

Everyday rules

  • Minimum two adults on site at all times — no adult alone out of sight with a child
  • Ratio: 1 adult to 7 children (max)
  • Toilets: children go in pairs; adults never alone with a child in the toilet area; any help given within sight of another adult
  • DBS: all staff hold current enhanced DBS checks — certificates seen and logged. Everyone reads and signs the safeguarding policy before day 1

Staff code of conduct — the short version

  • Avoid being alone with a child wherever possible
  • No personal contact with children — no personal numbers, messaging or social media
  • Photos taken by Nick only, on his own phone, and only with consent
  • Physical contact only when necessary, appropriate and proportionate
  • Report any concern, allegation or incident immediately — never dismiss one

Concern about a member of staff

Report it to Nick (DSO) straight away. If the concern is about Nick or you can't raise it internally, ring the NSPCC whistleblowing helpline: 0800 028 0285 or help@nspcc.org.uk.

Allegations against staff must also be reported to the Dorset LADO (see contacts) — normally within one working day.

Photos

Photos taken only by Nick, using his own phone. To keep this safe:

  • Check the consent list before taking any — respect any child who doesn't want to be photographed
  • Photos moved to the secure Drive folder and deleted from the phone at the end of each day
  • Never used for anything else while taking or holding camp photos, and never left in a general camera roll overnight
  • Never use full names alongside images
  • Consent can be withdrawn any time → delete that child's photos

Additional needs

Read each child's needs (and don't-needs) in the folder before day 1.

  • The printed contact/medical folder used on-site is accessible to all staff during a live incident, separate from the Nick-only digital access
🤝Values & Behaviour

Day 1: agree the values with the children so they own them. Our camp values:

Look after each other

  • Help younger children if they need support
  • If anyone looks or feels left out, bring them in
  • Give lots of encouragement if someone's feeling nervous
  • Help without being asked

Look after the camp

  • Take care of the equipment
  • Clean up after ourselves and keep rubbish in the bins
  • Be polite and considerate to staff and guests
  • Listen up when we need you to, especially for anything about safety

Give it a go

  • If something feels hard, keep going — that's how you get better at it
  • Practice, practice, practice
  • Ask for help when you need it
  • Try to get a little bit better at something today

Watch for anyone on the edge of a group or sitting out — actively invite them in, don't wait for them to ask.

Gold band

Never for sporting ability or winning. It's for character, effort and living out our values.

At the end of the day, each camp leader gives out their own gold band to a child who's gone above and beyond. Watch all day for children showing courage, kindness, teamwork, resilience or leadership — for example:

  • Encouraging another child who's feeling nervous
  • Persevering after finding an activity difficult
  • Helping without being asked
  • Excellent teamwork
  • Trying something new despite the nerves

Present it at the end-of-day reflection. Name the value and say exactly why that child was chosen — e.g. "today's band is for kindness — Maya saw someone struggling with their den and went over without being asked." The public reason is what teaches the rest of the camp.

Success here is participation, confidence and kindness — never sporting ability. Every child should leave proud of something.

3 strikes

  1. Reminder
  2. Quiet word away from the group
  3. Immediate phone call home — and log it
⚽Activities
Every activity: named leader, quick dynamic risk check (weather, ground, kit, mood), safety briefing, clear boundaries — and everyone stops on the whistle.

Football

Check the pitch first. No climbing on goals — portable goals checked they're secure. Soft/light balls, no blasting at close range.

Tennis

Spaced-out hitting areas so nobody swings into anyone. Rackets carried, never swung about. Balls collected only when the leader calls it.

Netball

Non-contact — no pushing, grabbing or barging. Check the post is stable and nobody hangs off it. Match children by size and confidence where you can.

Golf

One swinger at a time, everyone else well behind. Foam/soft balls and clubs. Nobody retrieves anything until the leader calls stop.

Rounders

Drop the bat, don't throw it — brief this every game. Nobody stands in the swing zone. Post area kept clear so runners don't collide with fielders.

Capture the flag

Open, visible space only. No hiding in woodland, behind buildings or anywhere near water. Set the no-go zones before play; staff watch the edges.

Forest school & crafts

Stone painting · leaf & flower printing · clay creatures · wood cookies · God's Eye weaving · den building

  • Don't pick or eat plants — Annika identifies what's safe to use
  • Sticks carried safely, never thrown
  • Headcounts before, during and after
  • Water areas out of bounds
  • Hands washed after every session
  • Den building: tarps and guy lines set low, lines marked so nobody trips, dens checked before children get inside

Leaf & flower printing uses hammers — it's a tool activity, so Annika leads it:

  • 1 adult to 4 children, gloves on, designated area with a safety bubble around each child
  • Safety points: second hand out of the way, tap or whack, look around before swinging
  • Hammers counted out and counted back in

Full detail per activity is in the Activity Risk Assessment 2026-27 in the Drive — read it before leading a session.

🍉Food & allergies
Nobody shares food. Ever.
  • Check the allergy list before every snack time
  • Hands washed (soap at the hand-wash station) before eating
  • Drinking water available all day — remind them in the heat

🌊Water & lakes
The lakes are NOT in use this camp. No lifeguards on site — the water is a hazard, not an activity.
  • Children never near the water's edge — agree and walk the boundary with them on day 1
  • Sessions set up well away from the lakes
  • Headcount before & after every move around the site
  • If a child is missing, the water's edge is checked first — see the emergency card

Sauna and paddle boards are off the programme. If that ever changes, insurance and a rescue plan get confirmed in writing first.

⛈️Wet weather
Default: we stay on site, whatever the weather. Serious conditions → lead decides.
  • Rain: carry on — shelter up, umbrellas by the toilet, indoor-style fillers ready (air-dry clay, wood cookies, wool crafts)
  • Severe weather or lightning: lead assesses — if it's not safe to stay, we look for an alternative venue if one's available (Litton Cheney Hall is the nearest option) or contact parents for early collection
  • Extreme heat: same approach — more shade, more water, less running about; if the site can't be kept safe, lead decides on venue change or early collection
  • Any change of plan: parents texted immediately with where and when
